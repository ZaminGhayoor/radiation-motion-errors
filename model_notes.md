# Model notes

## What is being modeled

An ideal rigid phantom moves laterally across a stationary, prescribed field. This provides a known reference against which reconstruction errors can be measured. It is inspired by the motion problem in radiation delivery, but the oscillator is not asserted to be a model of an individual patient's breathing.

All observations are generated by the code. No parameters were estimated from patient data or a machine. The main geometry is one-dimensional; there is no deformation, beam rotation, changing aperture, or respiratory gating. There is no photon transport, scatter calculation, buildup model, or tissue heterogeneity. The depth factor below is an assumed scaling, not a clinical depth-dose model.

## Motion and units

For mass $m$ and spring constant $k$:

$$m\ddot{x}=-kx,\qquad \omega=\sqrt{k/m},\qquad x(t)=A\cos(\omega t).$$

The code stores separate position and velocity states:

$$\frac{d}{dt}\begin{bmatrix}x\\v\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}v\\-\omega^2x\end{bmatrix}.$$

The analytic velocity and acceleration are $v=-A\omega\sin(\omega t)$ and $a=-A\omega^2\cos(\omega t)$. With $m=1$ kg and period 4 s, $k=m(2\pi/4)^2$ N/m. The numerical states use mm and mm/s. Convert them to metres before computing force in newtons or energy in joules.

Euler uses $y_{n+1}=y_n+h f(y_n)$. Heun averages the slope at the current state and an Euler prediction. Their observed global error orders approach one and two in the convergence table. For this oscillator, forward Euler artificially increases energy; its phase plot makes that visible. Heun also has energy drift and is not an energy-conserving integrator.

## Measurements and interpolation

The sensor samples $y_i=x(t_i)+b+\epsilon_i$, with independent $\epsilon_i\sim N(0,\sigma^2)$. The main experiment uses $b=0$; the error-budget experiment adds a separate 0.2 mm bias to the same noise realization.

Newton divided differences build a cubic polynomial from four nearby nodes. Changing stencils keeps the degree low and avoids a single high-degree global polynomial. The pieces agree in value at sample times, but their derivatives need not agree there. Do not assume the local Newton reconstruction is globally differentiable.

Natural cubic splines are twice continuously differentiable and impose $p''(t_0)=p''(t_n)=0$. That is a modeling choice. It disagrees with the exact acceleration at this benchmark's endpoints. The interior metric excludes the first and last sampling intervals.

For four exact samples of a sufficiently smooth function, the local cubic remainder is

$$x(t)-p_3(t)=\frac{x^{(4)}(\xi)}{4!}\prod_{j=0}^{3}(t-t_j).$$

Since $|x^{(4)}|\le A\omega^4$, this gives a deterministic interpolation bound on each stencil. This expression does not include sensor noise; interpolation passes that noise into the curve.

## Differentiation errors

At interior points of a uniform grid,

$$v_i\approx\frac{x_{i+1}-x_{i-1}}{2h},\qquad a_i\approx\frac{x_{i+1}-2x_i+x_{i-1}}{h^2}.$$

Both formulas have $O(h^2)$ truncation error for sufficiently smooth data. Independent measurement noise has variances

$$\operatorname{Var}(\widehat v)=\frac{\sigma^2}{2h^2},\qquad \operatorname{Var}(\widehat a)=\frac{6\sigma^2}{h^4}.$$

The factor six is $1^2+(-2)^2+1^2$. The acceleration experiment compares empirical RMSE from 4,000 noise draws with $\sqrt{\text{bias}^2+6\sigma^2/h^4}$ at each step size. This is sensor noise. The separate noiseless differentiation plot shows truncation and floating-point cancellation.

## Dose accumulation

Let $\Phi$ be the standard normal CDF. For half-width $w=7$ mm and penumbra scale $s=1.5$ mm, the dimensionless field is

$$B(\xi)=\Phi((\xi+w)/s)-\Phi((\xi-w)/s).$$

The prescribed dose rate is

$$q(\xi,z)=q_0 e^{-\mu z}B(\xi),$$

where $q_0=0.4$ Gy/s, $\mu=0.05$ cm$^{-1}$ and $z=5$ cm are illustrative. A material point initially at coordinate $r$ moves to $r+x(t)$, so its dose is

$$D(r)=\int_0^T q(r+x(t),z)\,dt.$$

Reference doses use the analytic motion with SciPy's adaptive quadrature at absolute and relative tolerances $10^{-11}$. Its returned error estimate is recorded, not treated as a proof. Reconstruction doses use a fixed trapezoid grid. The complete-period benchmark happens to give very small quadrature error; `quadrature_convergence.csv` uses a 7.3 s partial exposure to check the usual second-order behavior without that symmetry.

The signed error budget is evaluated at $r=6$ mm. Each term compares adjacent versions of the calculation: stationary, exact moving, exact motion on the integration grid, clean interpolated motion, noisy interpolated motion, then a biased sensor. Their sum equals the final difference. The decomposition is order-dependent because the dose map is nonlinear. Physical motion relative to a stationary assumption is a model difference, not an interpolation error.

A useful error-propagation bound follows from $|q'(\xi)|\le L_q$, where a valid bound for this prescribed profile is $L_q=q_0 e^{-\mu z}/(s\sqrt{2\pi})$:

$$|D_{\widehat x}(r)-D_x(r)|\le L_q\int_0^T|\widehat x(t)-x(t)|\,dt\le L_qT\|\widehat x-x\|_\infty.$$

This is a bound for exact time integrals under the same field model. Numerical quadrature introduces an additional error. A maximum evaluated only on a finite grid is not automatically the continuous supremum required by the theorem.

## Biological response and distinct states

The LQ response is $S(D)=\exp(-\alpha D-\beta D^2)$, with $\alpha$ in Gy$^{-1}$ and $\beta$ in Gy$^{-2}$. The examples use $(0.30,0.03)$ and $(0.15,0.05)$. These are assumed values, not estimates for named tissues. $S$ represents a modeled clonogenic surviving fraction; it is not a patient's survival probability or a prediction of treatment success.

The derivative explains local sensitivity:

$$S'(D)=-(\alpha+2\beta D)S(D),\qquad \Delta S\approx S'(D)\Delta D.$$

This use assumes an acute exposure and leaves out fractionation, proliferation, oxygen effects and repair during delivery. The default 8 s exposure is interpreted within that toy approximation.

The Arnoldi exercise uses an independent post-exposure compartment model:

$$H'=rR,\qquad R'=-(r+\ell)R,\qquad I'=\ell R.$$

Here $H$ has no modeled damage, $R$ has repairable damage and $I$ has irreversible damage. Rates are in min$^{-1}$. The initial condition is $R_0=1-e^{-cD}$, $H_0=1-R_0$, $I_0=0$, with assumed $c=0.25$ Gy$^{-1}$. Forty locations use repair rates from 0.01 to 0.06 min$^{-1}$, and $\ell=0.008$ min$^{-1}$. There is no exchange between locations. These choices make a tractable linear-system exercise; they do not reproduce the LQ model or describe measured repair kinetics.

The equations conserve $H+R+I$. The exact solution uses $R(t)=R_0e^{-(r+\ell)t}$, with the lost $R$ population partitioned between $H$ and $I$ in proportions $r/(r+\ell)$ and $\ell/(r+\ell)$.

## Arnoldi and error analysis

Stacking the states gives $u'=Ku$. Implicit Euler requires solving

$$(I-\Delta tK)u_{n+1}=u_n.$$

Writing $A=I-\Delta tK$, Arnoldi builds $AQ_k=Q_{k+1}\overline H_k$. With zero initial guess, GMRES finds $c$ minimizing $\|\|b\|_2 e_1-\overline H_kc\|_2$ and forms $u_k=Q_kc$. The code explicitly measures $b-Au_k$; the final coefficient alone is not used as the residual.

The experiment reports:

- Relative residual: $\|b-Au_k\|_2/\|b\|_2$.
- Relative forward error against a direct solve of the same discrete system.
- Orthogonality loss: $\|Q_k^TQ_k-I\|_F$.
- Scaled Arnoldi-relation error.
- The bound $\text{relative forward error}\le\kappa_2(A)\times\text{relative residual}$ in exact arithmetic.

Direct solutions carry floating-point error and are numerical references. The exact compartment solution separately measures time discretization error. Neither measure tells us the biological model error, which is unquantified here.

The matrix has only 120 states and a block structure that can be solved more simply. Keeping GMRES explicit makes the Krylov-space calculation inspectable. Useful extensions are a nontrivial spatial coupling model, sparse matrix-vector products, restarting, preconditioning, and studying how conditioning changes the residual/error relationship.
